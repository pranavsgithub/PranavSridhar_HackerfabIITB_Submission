## Reactive Ion Etching 
Link to Youtube Video: https://youtu.be/1PJZsOprbJo
Reactive Ion Etching is a type of dry etching. Dry etching is preferred in microfabrication because it is anisotropic, achieves well defined vertical features and causes no dredging/undercutting 

- RIE uses a radio frequency electric field to generate plasma, and processes gasses are passed through this plasma to generate free radicals, ions and electrons 
- Electrons that are generated by the plasma hit the wafer/substrate and cause a negative bias on the surface of the substrate 
- Positive ions are pulled by the negative bias and slam vertically into the surface. This physical impact **supplies the activation energy** needed to start and sustain the chemical reaction and occurs only at the spots of bombaring (perpendicular to the surface of the wafer), resulting in a perfect vertical etch

CCP-RIE 
- Capacitively Coupled Plasma (CCP) utilizes two parallel electrode plates inside a vacuum chamber to generate and sustain a plasma. A radio frequency (RF) voltage is applied to the wafer platter creating an alternating electric field between the plates that accelerates electrons back and forth to create the plasma. 
- Because the same RF power supply controls both the plasma density  and the DC self-bias , they are dependent on each other. Increasing the power to get a denser plasma automatically increases the ion energy, which can lead to high physical bombardment and  wafer damage. 
- CCP is traditionally used for applications requiring lower plasma densities and high directional ion energy, such as standard reactive-ion etching (RIE) and plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD).

ICP-RIE 
- Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP) systems overcome the limitations of CCP by using an external RF induction coil to generate plasma via electromagnetic induction. 
- The RF current passing through the coil creates a time-varying magnetic field, which induces a circular electric field inside the chamber that efficiently ionizes the gas without relying on electrode plates.
- ICP systems feature independent controls: the induction coil independently controls the plasma density, while a separate RF supply applied to the wafer platter independently controls the ion acceleration energy/DC self bias.
- This independent control allows incredibly fast chemical etch rates with high plasma densities while keeping ion energy low to minimize physical wafer damage, making ICP ideal for advanced, high-aspect-ratio applications like Deep RIE (DRIE).

CCP VS ICP RIE 
The etches hackerfab needs are simple and shallow, which are openings in 100-140nm thickness layer of oxide at 3-5 micrometre feature sizes, for which CCP RIE would suffice 
CCP RIE offers a massive cost reduction compared to ICP RIE. It is also easier to build compared to ICP RIE and can operate at higher pressures (10-100 mTorr as compared to 1mTorr and below) which makes it easier to implement the vacuum as well

Therefore, I choose CCP-RIE

## Complete Explanation of CCP-RIE 
### Setup 
- Two parallel electrodes in a vacuum chamber at about 10-100 mTorr
- Pass around 10-20 sccm (standard cubic centrimetre) of process gass through the vacuum chamber 
- The wafer lies on the lower electrode, which is connected to a 13.56 MHz RF supply with a blocking capacitor
- The upper electrode and the chamber walls are grounded.

## How the Plasma is generated 

- The applied RF creates an osciallting electric field between the two electrodes. A few free electrons are always present between these electrodes and the RF field acts upon them 
- The electrons oscillate in the field and gain some energy with each oscillation 
- The electrons eventually gain enough energy, as they can gain energy from the field without much interruption from gas molecules due to the low pressure, to knock out other electrons from the gas molecules, creating positive ions and electrons 
- This step keeps repeating, causing an avalanche effect causing a steady plasma 
- Electrons also hit molecules with less energy than ionization energy, but enough to break them into free radicals. This dissocation is what makes the reactive species: CHF₃ → CHF₂ + F (radical), and CF₄ → CF₃ + F (radical)
### Why ions are accelerated towards the wafer
- Electrons are much faster than ions due to their lower mass, so they reach the walls and electrodes first. The plasma is left at a positive potential relative to every surface. Next to each surface, a thin region with very few electrons forms, called a sheath. The sheath is negatively charged and attracts positive ions 
- Because of the blocking capacitor, the wafer electrode cannot pass any net DC current. On the positive part of each RF cycle it collects a burst of electrons, while on the negative part it collects much lesser ions. The capacitor stores the excess charge, so the electrode charges up negative until the average currents balance. The negative potential of the wafer electrode is called DC Self bias.
- The sheath field is perpendicular to the wafer. Ions are heavy and cannot follow a 13.56 MHz oscillation, so they respond to the average sheath voltage and strike the wafer with an energy of the plasma potential minus the self-bias 
- The ions catalyze the breakage of surface bonds, covered under next part 
### What causes material to be etched
Three reasons for material etching 
1) Chemical etching by radicals. Eg: in RIE of SiO2, fluorocarbon gases are used for reactive etching. Fluorine free radicals attack the unmasked areas of SiO2 layer and form SiF4, which is volatile and is removed by vacuum 
2) Ions also knock out surface atoms by momentum transfer. It is slow and unselective and isnt the major reason for etching 
3) Ion-enhanced etching is the main reason. Ion bombardment breaks surface bonds and supplies the energy for radicals to react and for products to leave. 

### Parameters controlling RIE 
1) Pressure: The chemical parts of the etching are anisotropic, and the perpendicular bombardment of positive ions (ion enhanced etching) is responsible mainly for the vertical etching, as pressure is increased, ion enchanced etching is supressed due to collissions between the ions and the gas molecules, causing chemical etching to dominate and higher isotropic etching due to chemical radical reactions 
2) RF power: Increasing RF power increases plasma density and DC self bias both. The etch rate rises with increasing RF power and increasing the RF power too much can cause damage to the wafer and stripping away of areas marked by photoresist as well (Knocking out of surface atoms)
  3) Gas chemistry: The process gas is responsible for the free radicals being generated and chemical reaction
     - The nature of the products formed control the result of the etching, for example for etching of Silicon dioxide, using fluorine rich gases such as CF4 creates volatile compounds (SiF4) that are directly removed by vacuum, while using hydrogen containing fluorocarbons like CHF3 leads to polymer deposition which can be treater chemically later 


## Proposed RIE Tool 
#### Overview 
The RIE Tool is a metal chamber consisting of a two chamber setup with a showerhead for gas dispersal at the top electrode and wafer on the a smaller bottom electrode which will be powered by the rf source.
The inflow setup includes the gas cylinders, regulators for the cylinders, mass flow controllers and the showerhead electrode 
The gas flows from the gas cylinders when the regulators are open and the mass flow controllers are used to control the rate of the gas flowing in 
A 13.56 MHz supply, through a matching network and blocking capacitor, drives the lower electrode.

### Chamber and electrode arrangement
The chamber would be a cylindrical aluminium (or SS304) chamber, about 250-300 mm in diameter and 150-200 mm tall, with a lift-off lid sealed by a rubber gasket for vacuum. Glass cannot be used since fluorine radicals can etch off quartz and glass 
The electrodes would be two paralell plates 4-6 cm apart, lower electrode to be of diameter 100-150mm which would comfortably accomodate a 4in silicon wafer. The top electrode needs to be of a larger area (Can be made to just fit into the chamber)
The wafer would be placed on the bottom electrode using a silicon or alumina cover plate 
A grounded ring can be placed right under and to the sides of the lower electrode to prevent ion etching at places where plasma might reach unintentionally 
For cooling, a water cooling system can be implemented on the lower electrode for cooling the setup 
Justifications: 1) The top electrode is made of larger area as sheath thickness is inversely proportional to the area of the surface in contact with the plasma so for a good sheath layer on the wafer, we would need to make other surfaces of large areas 
2) SS340 or Al6063 or other common Al grades can grow protective layers against radical etching (AlF3 and AlF3-Carbofluoride polymers) as compared to glass that would get etched by plasma 
3) Rubber gaskets are enough at this stage because the vacuum is not very high, alternate arrangements would be needed for vacuum < 1mTorr 
### Vacuum pumping and pressure control
The chosen pump is a rotary vane pump system, of 10-15 m^3/hour capacity, either run dry or with an inert oil based coolant/sealant
for the valve a, manual throttle valve between the chamber and the pump can be used
The measuring gauges chosen are a capacitance manometer (about 0.1-1 Torr range) for process pressure, and a Pirani gauge for roughing and base pressure, which is below about 10 mTorr, with a leak-up test before every run. The process pressure is 20-100 mTorr

Justifications: 1) Rotary valve pump is chosen due to its inexpensive + medium-to-high vacuum nature and the fact that it can be run dry for long periods of time 
2)Capacitance manometer is required for accurate pressure sensing of gas mixtures as pirani gauge depends on thermal conductivity of gas

#### Pressure & Capacity Calculations: 
In steady state, amount of gas going in = amount of gas coming out 
The formula is Q = S × p
Q is throughput, the amount of gas flowing through per second.
S is the effective pumping speed, the volume the pump clears per second at the chamber.
p is the chamber pressure.
consider 10 sccm flow = 0.127 Torr·L/s
S = 3 Litre/second
p = around 42 mtorr 
3 litre per second is around 10-15 m^3/hour which is the capacity of most commerically available rotary pumps 

### RF and Plasma Delivery 
13.56 MHz solid-state supply is chosen as the generator with 300 W maximum, 50 Ω output. It would be operated between 50-200W 
Matching network: an L-type network with two variable capacitors and an inductor, tuned by hand in v1 for minimum reflected power. 
A blocking capacitor in series with the powered electrode creates the self-bias.
A power meter will need to be implemented to measure the power draw 
A bias monitor setup can be done using a a DC voltmeter on the electrode through an RF choke, reading the self-bias.
Shielding and grounding: The RF line and matching box are placed in a grounded metal enclosure 

Justifications: 1) Frequency is 13.56Mhz because it is the most commonly available frequency in industry, internal resistance of 50 ohm is also standard 2) The plasma's impedance varies highly and can cause power reflections which can cause the RF circuitry to flow or damage to the wafer + vessel so the matching network is implemented 

### Process gas delivery
For the initial setup, consider only CHF₃, Ar, O₂ , each from a cylinder with a regulator.
Control: one mass flow controller per gas, ranges of about 20-50 sccm. MFCs will need to be calibrated per gas. Stainless steel or fluoropolymer tubing with compression fittings must be used for all lines
Injection is done at the grounded upper electrode which is a showerhead, plate with many small holes, so gas reaches the wafer evenly.
An N₂ line can be attached to act as a purge to vent the chamber and flush the lines.

### Controls and Safety 
In the initial version, everything is manually checked, the process flow must be strictly followed 
purge chamber -> place wafer -> purge again -> start vacuum pump -> establish vacuum baseline -> pass process gas -> check for any leaks anywhere in the entire pipeline -> check for proper grounding of RF supply -> turn on RF supply -> monitor power usage -> turn off RF at stipulated time -> turn off process gas supply -> let the chamber cool to room temperature -> purge N2 gas -> Open chamber -> Retrieve sample 

#### Hazards and precautions taken: 
Toxic gas products (HF, COF₂, SiF₄) -> Exhaust through a trap and a scrubber system which is a bubbler with water or alkaline solution into a fume hood or open atmosphere.Calcium gluconate gel must be kept HF exposure, and gloves and eye protection must be worn 
For RF burns and interference, Shielded, grounded enclosure and lid must be implemented, and no exposed RF parts must be present
High voltage on the matching network calls for an enclosed box with an interlocked cover
Gas cylinders must be secured, valves must be closed at all times and leak check must be done at every joint

### Watercooling system 
Since the bottom electrode with the wafer would easily reach temperatures of 100-200 degrees celcius and above, a small watercooling pump based system can be implemented. 
- A spiral channel of diameter 8-10mm and total length 300-400mm can be made inside the aluminum electode 
- It can be connected to PTFE piping and a small water based cooler of capacity around 25litres 
- A flow rate of 0.5-1Litre/min should be good enough for cooling the electrode 
## Representative Etch Process
The thermal furnace can be used to grow oxide layers on Silicon substrated which can be used as the substrate for etching 
S1813 photoresist can be applied and the existing DLP based litho can be used for patterning the photoresist. Baked part of photoresist can be removed using acetone + IPA or NMP 
CHF3 is chosen as the etchant due to its tendency to form polymer with Si and other substrates but etches away SiO2 

List of chosen conditions-

Pressure	- about 40-50 mTorr
Ar - adjust to achieve 40-50 mTorr pressure
